Cuisine type — within restaurants

Does the type of cuisine matter too?

We focused on the restaurant category — over 20,000 locations — and asked: does the type of food a restaurant serves predict its inspection outcome? To find out, we looked at each restaurant's most recent inspection only, so every restaurant is counted exactly once.

⚠
How we classified cuisines — and why it is imprecise. The dataset has no "cuisine type" column. Instead, we searched each restaurant's name for keywords: a restaurant is counted as "Pizza" if the word pizza appears in its name, "Indian" if indian appears, and so on. This works reasonably well for some types — pizza and kebab restaurants almost always say so in their name — but poorly for others. Many Italian restaurants do not say "Italian"; many Chinese restaurants do not say "Chinese." This means cuisines that do use their cuisine name consistently (e.g. Indian) will appear larger and may show inflated risk, simply because the sample is more complete. Treat these results as exploratory, not definitive.

What "relative risk" means: we calculated what percentage of restaurants in each group had any inspection issue (Score 2 or higher) at their most recent visit. We then divided that by the overall restaurant average of 2.65%. A value of 1.0× = exactly average. 2.0× = twice as likely to have an issue as a typical restaurant. Restaurant counts are shown in brackets.
Each restaurant is counted exactly once using its most recent inspection only. Relative risk = (share of that cuisine group with Score ≥ 2) ÷ (2.65% restaurant-wide average). Only cuisines with ≥ 30 keyword-matched restaurants are shown. Cuisines identified by keyword in business name — see methodology note above.

Highest observed risk
Indian, Mexican, Thai, and Kebab restaurants all show ≥1.8× the average risk of any inspection issue — though note the caveat on name-based classification above.
△
Close to average
Burgers, cafés, Italian, and Vietnamese restaurants cluster around 1.1–1.4× the average — elevated but not dramatically so.
↘
Lowest observed risk
Sushi/Japanese and Chinese restaurants perform below the restaurant average — at 0.93× and 0.88× respectively — and had zero serious (score 4) outcomes in this dataset.

Cuisine type adds nuance within the restaurant world. But it's worth zooming out again: how do the largest multi-location operators — the chains that run tens or hundreds of branches — compare to each other? Scale should bring resources, training, and systems. Does it show up in the inspections?
